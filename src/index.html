How to use

  1. Start audio and allow the microphone. Pick Dante Virtual Soundcard (or your USB interface) as Output, and your measurement mic as Input — a USB mic/interface, or Dante Virtual Soundcard with the mic routed to DVS receive channel 1. Set the macOS mic mode to Standard (no Voice Isolation). If a device is missing, press ↻ Refresh.
  2. List each speaker / mix send, the output channel that feeds it, and its type. Enter what delay is in the console now (0 if none).
  3. Put the mic where the speakers you’re aligning overlap (for a delay speaker: where its coverage meets the mains; for subs: a typical listening spot, ideally 2–3 spots).
  4. Auto plays pink noise into each send in turn and measures every speaker in one go. Manual plays one noise feed and you unmute one speaker at a time at the console and tell the app which one is on.
  5. Type the big numbers into your console. Measure again to verify: everything should read ≈0 change.

iPhone as a wireless mic: open this page on the iPhone (it needs an https:// address), choose “This device only listens”, tap Listen, then on the Mac press “Play sequence only”. No cable or network link between the two is needed; the app removes the clock difference between the devices. Or record the sequence with Voice Memos, AirDrop it to the Mac, and use “Analyze a recording”.

How it computes: the same dual-channel FFT method Smaart uses. Transfer function H = Gxy/Gxx against a known pink-noise reference (or a loop-back), complex averaging with coherence, impulse response by inverse FFT, and a delay finder on the impulse-response peak (first strong arrival). Subs are aligned by phase at the crossover, choosing the delay and polarity that sum loudest across a 1.5-octave band.
